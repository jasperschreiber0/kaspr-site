# CRO Audit: Luna Lash Bar — Homepage

**Date:** 2026-06-28
**Page:** Homepage (hypothetical)
**Goal:** Fill mid-week appointment gaps, grow Instagram following, upsell brow treatments to existing lash clients
**ICP:** Women 25–40, inner-city Melbourne, book every 4–6 weeks, high disposable income

---

## Overall Conversion Verdict

The homepage does one thing well: it gets a visitor to a booking button. That's the floor, not the ceiling. Everything else — the headline, services list, absence of social proof, and zero acknowledgement of the brow offer — is leaving money on the table.

A woman who already knows Luna Lash Bar is likely to hit "Book Now" regardless. The page is not doing nearly enough for the warm-but-not-converted visitor: someone who found the business through Instagram, a friend's recommendation, or a Google search. That visitor lands, sees a generic headline, a list with no prices, and no evidence that anyone else loves this place — and she bounces to check out a competitor who makes her feel something.

The mid-week gap and brow upsell goals are entirely invisible on the current page. There is no mechanism on this homepage to nudge timing or introduce the brow menu to someone who only came for lashes.

**What is working:**
- Single, clear CTA ("Book Now") with a direct path to Fresha — no dead ends
- Model photography sets the visual tone
- Service range is comprehensive

**What is not working:**
- Headline is a claim without proof or personality
- No pricing, not even indicative ranges
- No testimonials, reviews, or social proof of any kind
- No Instagram presence surfaced anywhere
- Brow treatments buried in a plain list alongside lash services — no separate call-out
- No mid-week incentive or urgency mechanism
- Footer-only contact info — feels like an afterthought

---

## 1. Hero Section

### Headline: "Melbourne's Favourite Lash Bar"

**The issue:** This is a claim, not a conversation. "Favourite" is unsubstantiated — there is no review count, award, or testimonial to back it up. It also speaks about Luna Lash Bar rather than speaking to the woman reading it. She is not thinking about who Melbourne's favourite is; she is thinking about whether her lashes will look incredible for her next three weeks.

The ICP has high standards and limited time. She wants the outcome, not the credential.

**Rewrite options:**

Option A (outcome-focused):
> Lashes that last. Brows that frame. Done properly, in the CBD.

Option B (personality-led, confidence of the brand voice):
> Your next set is going to be your best set.

Option C (specific + social proof baked in):
> 5-star lash extensions and brow treatments in Melbourne's CBD — booked by women who don't settle.

**Recommendation:** Option B for warmth and confidence; pair it with a subheadline that adds the functional detail (location, services, guarantee if one exists).

---

### Subheadline: "Expert lash extensions and brow treatments in the CBD."

**The issue:** Functional but flat. "Expert" is self-declared. "In the CBD" is good — it signals convenience for this ICP — but the rest is just a description. It does not tell her what makes Luna the right choice, or why she should book today rather than next week.

**Rewrite:**
> Volume, Hybrid, Classic — plus brow lamination and tints, all under one roof. No rushing. No compromising. Book in under 60 seconds.

This version names the services (removes ambiguity), signals the quality experience, and removes friction anxiety around the booking process.

---

### CTA Button: "Book Now"

**The issue:** "Book Now" is functional but generic. It tells her what to do, not what she gets. For a service with a premium price point, the CTA is an opportunity to reinforce the value.

**Rewrite options:**
- "Book My Appointment" (personal, ownership)
- "Book a Set" (lash-industry language, feels insider)
- "See Available Times" (low-commitment framing, reduces hesitation)

**Recommendation:** Test "Book My Appointment" against "Book Now." The possessive language ("my") has consistently outperformed generic CTAs in service bookings. Low effort, potentially meaningful lift.

**Secondary CTA to add:**
Consider adding a secondary, lower-commitment CTA alongside the booking button:
> "Follow us on Instagram" — links to @lunalashbar

This serves the Instagram growth goal for visitors who are not yet ready to book but are interested enough to stay connected.

## 2. Services Section

### Current state: plain bulleted list, no prices

**The issue:** A list of service names without prices or descriptions asks the visitor to do all the work. For this ICP — high disposable income, time-poor, used to quality experiences — a plain bullet list reads as low-effort. More critically, it provides zero help in choosing. A first-time visitor does not know the difference between Classic, Hybrid, and Volume. She may not know what Henna Brows are. Without guidance, she either bounces to research elsewhere or books the cheapest option out of uncertainty, reducing average order value.

**What to add:**

1. **Brief descriptor for each service** — one line maximum.

   Example:
   - Classic Lashes — Natural and polished. Perfect for an everyday look.
   - Hybrid Lashes — The best of both. Fuller than Classic, softer than Volume.
   - Volume Lashes — Full, dramatic, and impossible to ignore.
   - Lash Lift — Your natural lashes, curled and lifted. No extensions needed.
   - Brow Lamination — Fluffy, structured brows that last up to 6 weeks.
   - Brow Tint — Deeper colour, more definition. Takes 20 minutes.
   - Henna Brows — Tint that stains the skin as well as the hair, for bolder results.

2. **Indicative pricing** — even a "from $X" signal prevents the hidden-cost anxiety that kills bookings.

   Example: "Classic Lashes — from $85 | Hybrid — from $110 | Volume — from $130"

   This is not about revealing every price point. It's about removing the "is this in my budget?" question that makes a visitor leave before she books.

3. **Visual separation between lash and brow services** — currently they sit in the same list. This is a missed upsell opportunity addressed in section 6 below.

---

## 3. Social Proof

### Current state: none

**The issue:** This is the single biggest conversion blocker on the page. There is no testimonial, no Google Review score, no "loved by X women in Melbourne," no before-and-after imagery, no staff names or credentials. For a service that requires a stranger putting tools near someone's eyes, trust is everything.

The ICP books lash appointments with someone she trusts — either through a referral or through a body of evidence that builds confidence. The current homepage offers none of that evidence.

**What to add, in priority order:**

1. **Star rating + review count above the fold or immediately below the hero.**

   Example: "4.9 stars — 312 Google Reviews" with a link to the Google listing. If a Fresha review score exists, use both. Place this directly beneath the hero CTA button.

2. **Two to three testimonials, specific and attributed.**

   These should name the service, the outcome, and ideally the suburb or life context.

   Example:
   > "I came in for a Hybrid set before a work trip and left feeling like a different person. Still going strong at week five. Luna is the only place I'll ever go."
   > — Sarah M., Fitzroy

   > "Had my brows laminated here after seeing the results on Instagram. My morning routine is basically nothing now. Worth every dollar."
   > — Jade T., South Yarra

   If real reviews exist on Google or Fresha, pull the best two or three verbatim. Do not fabricate.

3. **Before-and-after imagery** for both lashes and brows. This is the native content format of lash and brow businesses. If the Instagram feed is full of it (which it should be), one or two hero images pulled to the homepage would do significant work.

4. **Staff names and faces** — even a single line like "Meet your artists" with a photo and first name humanises the experience and builds trust before a stranger books an appointment.

---

## 4. Mid-Week Booking Gap

### Current state: no mechanism to address timing

**The issue:** The homepage makes no distinction between Tuesday and Saturday. For a business that wants to fill Tuesday–Thursday slots specifically, the page needs a hook that creates a reason to book mid-week rather than waiting for the weekend rush.

**What to add:**

1. **A mid-week offer section or banner** — this does not need to be permanent. It could rotate seasonally or quarterly.

   Example banner (beneath the hero, or as a sticky top bar):
   > Monday to Thursday appointments available this week — same great service, more breathing room. Book a weekday slot.

   Or with an incentive:
   > Book any Tuesday–Thursday appointment and add a Brow Tint for $20. Slots this week are going fast.

2. **Show real-time or near-real-time availability signals.** Fresha supports embedded availability calendars. Even a line like "Next available: Tuesday 2 July at 11am — Book it" creates urgency and moves a dithering visitor to action.

3. **Framing mid-week as a luxury, not a consolation.** The ICP responds to exclusivity and experience, not desperation. Language like "fewer clients, more attention, your artist's full focus" reframes a quiet Tuesday as premium, not a leftover slot.

## 5. Brow Upsell Opportunity

### Current state: brow services buried in a mixed list

**The issue:** Brow Lamination, Brow Tints, and Henna Brows sit in the same plain list as six lash services. A client who books lashes every four weeks and has never thought about brows will scan this list and see nothing that speaks to her. There is no visual hierarchy, no cross-sell prompt, no "why not add brows while you're here" moment.

This is a goal the client has named — grow brow revenue from existing lash clients — and the current page does nothing to serve it.

**What to add:**

1. **A dedicated brow section with a distinct heading** — physically separated from the lash services list.

   Example heading: "While you're here — your brows deserve attention too."

   Or more direct: "Most of our lash clients add a brow treatment. Here's why."

2. **A specific cross-sell callout** — either a banner or a styled card — targeting existing lash clients.

   Example:
   > Already booking lashes? Add Brow Lamination in the same appointment and wake up with a full face — no extra trip required. Add it when you book.

3. **Before-and-after brow imagery** — separate from lash imagery. Brow transformations are visually dramatic and are the highest-performing organic content for brow businesses. One strong before-and-after on the homepage, with a CTA to "Add Brows to My Booking," would do more than any copy change.

4. **Fresha upsell note** — confirm whether Fresha allows add-on services at booking. If it does, reference this in the CTA: "You can add a Brow Tint directly when you book your lashes." Reducing the steps to a yes removes the last objection.

## 6. Quick Wins

Changes that cost nothing and can be done this week:

**1. Rewrite the hero headline.**
Replace "Melbourne's Favourite Lash Bar" with a version that speaks to the ICP's outcome or confidence. Suggested: "Your next set is going to be your best set." No design changes required — it's a text swap.

**2. Add a star rating beneath the CTA button.**
Pull the Google Review score and count (e.g., "4.9 stars — 312 reviews") and add it as a one-line trust signal directly below the "Book Now" button. This is a copy addition only.

**3. Add a secondary Instagram CTA to the hero.**
Below or alongside the booking button, add: "Follow us on Instagram @lunalashbar" with a link. Zero design cost, directly serves the Instagram growth goal.

**4. Add one-line descriptors to each service.**
This is a content edit to the services list. No design work needed — just a sentence per service that tells the visitor what it is and who it's for.

**5. Add indicative pricing to the services list.**
Even "from $85" per service removes the pricing anxiety that kills first-time bookings. This is a copy addition to the existing services list.

---

## 7. Bigger Plays

Changes worth planning and building:

**1. Embed an Instagram feed in the homepage.**
This serves two goals at once: social proof (real client results, real energy) and Instagram growth (visitors see the account and follow). Most website builders support an Instagram embed widget. This is a development task but a light one. Place it between the services section and the footer, with a heading like "See what we've been creating."

**2. Add a dedicated brow section with cross-sell messaging.**
This is a content and design task: visually separate brow services from lash services, add a styled callout for existing lash clients, and include a before-and-after brow image. This is the most direct way to serve the brow upsell goal from the homepage.

**3. Add a mid-week availability prompt using Fresha's embed or API.**
If Fresha supports it, show the next available mid-week slot dynamically on the homepage ("Next Tuesday availability: 10am, 1pm, 3pm — Book now"). This creates genuine urgency without manufactured scarcity and directly addresses the mid-week booking gap goal. This is a development task and may require Fresha API access.
